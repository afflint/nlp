Track 6
Inside the Model
Mechanistic interpretability of a phenomenon

How does a model implement a specific behaviour, and can we test our explanation with interventions on its internals?

Possible perspectives
  • Linguistic phenomena: agreement, coreference, negation, tense, number
  • Factual recall and relations: where does the model retrieve a fact?
  • Pattern copying and induction: in-context learning, repetition
  • Simple reasoning or arithmetic: which components compute?
  • Behavioural tendencies: sycophancy, refusal, sentiment
  • Representations: directions, subspaces, features: can they be manipulated?
A good project has
  • A measurable phenomenon: controlled inputs, e.g. a logit difference
  • A falsifiable hypothesis: stated in advance, with what would refute it
  • Causal evidence: ablation, patching or steering, not only probes
  • Controls and declared limits: random components, corrupted inputs, many prompts
Common pitfalls
  • Attention maps or probe accuracies presented as an explanation
  • A phenomenon with no clear, measurable outcome
  • A hypothesis verified on one single prompt template
Search keywordsmechanistic interpretability, circuits, activation patching, causal tracing, logit lens, probing, attribution, induction heads, sparse autoencoders, superposition, steering vectors, representation engineering, faithfulness of explanations.
Feasibility and resourcesSmall models (GPT-2 sized, Pythia, a few hundred million parameters) and libraries such as TransformerLens are enough. The cost grows quickly with larger models. Unlike Track 3, causality here is inside the model.
